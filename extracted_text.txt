Objective: 
The aim of these activities is to build students’ knowledge in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and 
Machine Learning (ML) by exposing them to real-world applications in diverse sectors, such as 
healthcare, finance, and gaming. Through peer learning, expert sessions, and practical workshops, 
students will enhance their technical and problem-solving skills. These initiatives also focus on 
career readiness by providing guidance on placements, entrepreneurship, and participation in 
national-level hackathons, helping students prepare for success in the fast-evolving AI/ML 
industry. 
Following activities are done during Academic Year 2024-25 Even Semester:

discussions, demonstrations, and shared insights, the session created a platform for collaborative 
exploration of Industry 4.0 concepts.

Report on PSR Activity: Super AI Community Stakeholder Connect 
  
Objective:  
Designed to equip the next generation of tech professionals, these immersive learning experiences bring 
together industry mentors and peer-driven collaboration to deepen students' understanding of cutting-
edge fields such as Agentic AI, blockchain ecosystems, and the ever-evolving landscape of data science. 
From aptitude-based assessments and hands-on technical workshops to career navigation sessions and 
real-world tool exposure, every component is thoughtfully curated to sharpen logical thinking, 
strengthen quantitative reasoning, and build the kind of practical, implementation-ready skills that 
today's AI and ML-driven industries demand. Far beyond traditional classroom instruction, this 
initiative bridges the gap between academic foundations and professional expectations, empowering 
participants to step confidently into roles across research institutions, technology enterprises, and the 
rapidly expanding frontier of intelligent systems.

Activity 8: Hands-On Workshop on IoT x ML 
 
Date: 09 October 2025 
Time: 03:15 PM - 05:15 PM 
Mode: Offline  
Venue : Lab 307 
Speaker: Mrs.Poonam Gawade - Founder Tantraniketan 
 
Participants: ST and TT Students 
Objectives: 
 To introduce students to the evolution of IoT from IoP (Internet of People) to modern 
interconnected systems. 
 To help students understand real-life IoT applications and encourage brainstorming for 
innovative use cases. 
 To provide clarity on the IoT implementation flow—from hardware to connectivity to AI/ML 
integration. 
 To create awareness about IoT manufacturing challenges in India, including semiconductor 
issues. 
 To motivate students toward hands-on learning by offering opportunities for real-world IoT 
exposure. 
Description : 
The session began with an introductory talk by the speaker, Mr. Nitin, who provided a clear overview 
of the evolution of IoT, starting from the earlier concept of the Internet of People (IoP) to the modern 
Internet of Things (IoT). He discussed how IoT has grown into a powerful ecosystem connecting 
devices, data, and intelligence. 
To engage the audience, he asked students to share real-life IoT applications they were familiar with. 
This led to active brainstorming, helping students expand their thought process, creativity, and 
innovative abilities. The speaker further showcased a video demonstrating a highly advanced IoT-
enabled environment where everything from streetlights to home appliances, refrigerators to doors, and 
chimneys to room systems operates seamlessly through interconnected smart devices. 
Mr. Nitin also explained the complete flow of implementing IoT systems, starting from the hardware 
layer, moving to IoT connectivity, and finally integrating AI/ML models for intelligent automation. 
He highlighted important insights about IoT manufacturing, comparing the industry landscape of India 
and China. He also discussed the semiconductor manufacturing challenges in India, the impact of 
weather conditions on hardware production, and how these issues are addressed by importing 
semiconductor wafers. 
 
 
Towards the end, the speaker invited interested students to visit his office in Malad West for further 
hands-on learning and deeper discussions. He encouraged small groups of 4–5 students to interact, 
explore IoT applications, understand integration processes, and gain practical exposure.
